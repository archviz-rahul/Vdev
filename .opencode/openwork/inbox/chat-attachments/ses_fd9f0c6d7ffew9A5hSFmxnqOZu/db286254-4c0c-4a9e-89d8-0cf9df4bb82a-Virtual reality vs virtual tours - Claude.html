Analysis of the Audit Report

The report identifies a platform that is functionally complete but architecturally disorganised. Every feature works. Nothing is broken at the code level. The problem is entirely one of structure, discoverability, and navigation clarity — which means fixes are low-risk and high-impact.

The issues fall into four categories.

Category 1 — Hidden pages (highest impact). 26 pages across the AR and XR systems exist in the codebase but have no navigation entry points. A user visiting the site has no way to discover /ar, /ar/projects, /xr, /xr/editor, or any of their sub-pages. Category 2 — Duplicate routes (medium impact). The user dashboard exists at four different URLs. Streaming exists at five. The admin sidebar lists Media Library twice and Navigation Menus twice. Each duplicate adds cognitive load and creates ambiguity about which is the "real" version.

Category 3 — Mislabelled navigation (medium impact). The "VR Tours" header link points to /vr (the VR dashboard) instead of /vr/tours (the actual tours list). A visitor clicking it lands on a management dashboard rather than the experience they expected.

Category 4 — Unlinked sidebar items (low impact). Three pages — /vr/ai-guide, /vr/floor-plans, and /xr/help — exist but are not linked from their respective dashboards.

The Three Things That Matter Now

1. First Real Client — This Week

The most valuable thing you can do right now is put the platform in front of one real architect, developer, or real estate firm and walk them through it live. Not a polished demo — a working session where they upload a model, you set up hotspots together, and they share the link with someone on their team. This single session will surface more useful feedback than any amount of internal testing, and it creates your first case study.

You already have the Pixel Streaming infrastructure — a booked demo session through that system is a high-value, high-impressiveness entry point for premium clients.

2. Validate the Core Flow End-to-End

Before inviting paying clients, run through the complete journey using each of the three test accounts. Specifically: an admin uploads a GLB model, places hotspots, generates a client share link, then log in as the client and confirm the VR experience works on both a desktop browser and a mobile device. 3. Define the Offer and the Price

VizTR needs a clear answer to the question "what do I get and what does it cost?" You have enough built to offer at minimum two things: a project-based service (VizTR creates and hosts an XR experience for a client's specific project) and a platform subscription (architects get access to the tools to build their own). Week 3 — Pricing and Positioning. Add a clear pricing page to the website. Even rough tiers (Starter / Studio / Enterprise) create legitimacy and help prospects self-qualify. Update the services pages to reflect the full XR platform, not just the visualisation studio framing.

Week 4 — First Paid Engagement. Convert one of the outreach conversations into a paid project or a pilot subscription. Document the entire workflow so it can be repeated.

What to Build After That
